# HR Process Optimization at Synergy Enterprises — Business Case & SDLC

**Project type:** Business Analysis Capstone — Business Case + SDLC methodology selection
**Scale:** Global — 75,000+ employees across five continents
**Recommended investment:** $20.68M over 3 years

## Background
Synergy Enterprises runs fragmented, region-specific HR systems across its global workforce. The capstone develops the full business case for replacing them with a single, unified, cloud-based Global Human Capital Management (HCM) platform.

## Problem statement
Synergy's fragmented HR technology is creating six compounding issues:
- **Fragmented employee data** — scattered across legacy/regional systems, no single global workforce view
- **Inefficient HR operations** — manual/semi-automated onboarding, reviews, and reconciliation create overhead and errors
- **Lack of global HR insights** — inconsistent data blocks reliable reporting on workforce demographics, talent, and diversity
- **Inconsistent employee experience** — HR processes vary by location, weakening the global employer brand
- **Compliance and risk exposure** — managing diverse global labor laws and data privacy rules across fragmented systems is complex and risky
- **High IT maintenance cost** — many parallel HR systems consume budget that could fund higher-value work

## Objectives
Standardize HR processes globally · implement a unified HCM platform (recruitment, onboarding, payroll, performance, learning, workforce planning) · improve data accuracy via a single source of truth · modernize employee/manager self-service · reduce HR operating costs through automation and consolidation · enable strategic workforce planning with real-time analytics.

## Options considered
| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Maintain current systems (do nothing) | Not sustainable — continued inefficiency, compliance risk, cannot scale |
| Upgrade existing regional systems | Partial benefit only — doesn't fix fragmentation, limits global reporting, higher long-term cost |
| **Implement a unified global HCM platform (recommended)** | Single source of truth, standardized processes, better compliance/reporting/employee experience, lower long-term operating cost |

## Financial analysis
**3-year investment (USD):** $10.34M (Yr 1) + $6.6M (Yr 2) + $3.74M (Yr 3) = **$20.68M total** — covering HCM licensing, implementation/SI partner fees, internal programme resources, data migration, change management/training, infrastructure/integration, and a 10% contingency.

**3-year benefits (USD):** $2.5M (Yr 1) → $7.05M (Yr 2) → $11.4M (Yr 3) — driven by HR headcount redeployment, reduced system/licence costs, self-service productivity gains, reduced recruitment agency fees, compliance/risk cost avoidance, and improved retention.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Net Present Value (NPV) | **$15.6M** |
| 3-year ROI | **~75%** |
| Payback period | **~19 months** post go-live |
| Internal Rate of Return (IRR) | **~42%** |
| Annual run-rate savings (Year 3+) | $11.4M |

Benefits overtake annual cost during Year 2, turning cash-positive on a cumulative basis by month 19 post go-live — driven mainly by headcount redeployment and licence consolidation as legacy systems are decommissioned.

## Risk register
| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Resistance to change | High | Strong change management and stakeholder engagement |
| Data migration errors | Medium | Rigorous testing; phased migration |
| Integration challenges | Medium | Use experienced implementation partners |
| Compliance concerns | High | Ensure platform meets global data privacy standards |
| Cost overruns | Medium | Clear governance and scope control |

## Implementation roadmap
A 24-month, 5-phase rollout gated by 4 sign-offs (vendor selection, design approval, UAT, full regional go-live):
1. **Initiation (0–3 mo):** project governance, requirements gathering, vendor selection
2. **Design (3–6 mo):** global HR process standards, integration architecture, data migration strategy
3. **Build & Test (6–12 mo):** configure HCM modules, integrate with payroll/ERP, UAT
4. **Deployment (12–18 mo):** phased regional rollout, training/communication, data migration and go-live
5. **Stabilization & Optimization (18–24 mo):** monitor adoption, optimize workflows, decommission legacy systems

## SDLC methodology — hybrid Water-Scrum-Fall
Recommended a **hybrid methodology**: Waterfall-style phase gates govern the overall programme, while Agile/Scrum sprints run inside Build & Test and Deployment.

**Why not pure Waterfall:** This is a $10.3M Year-1 investment going to a global board, with data migration complexity across inconsistent legacy systems and five continents of regional variation. Locking requirements at Design and not revisiting them until UAT risks discovering region-specific gaps only after Build is complete — exactly the expensive rework this business case aims to eliminate.

**Why not pure Agile/Scrum:** This is COTS platform configuration (not ground-up development), governed by a fixed board-approved budget, a vendor contract, and compliance sign-offs that must land at defined points — hard to govern under a pure Scrum approach with no fixed upfront scope.

**Why hybrid fits:** Fixed phase gates handle board governance, budget control, and vendor/compliance milestones; 2–3 week sprints (with regional HR as product owners) handle HCM module configuration; each region's go-live runs as its own sprint cycle so lessons from Region 1 improve Region 2; data migration is validated in iterative batches tested each sprint rather than one big-bang cutover; and regional teams see working configurations early, giving feedback before go-live rather than only at final UAT. This mirrors the deployment pattern used by major HCM/ERP vendors (SAP Activate, Oracle OUM, Workday), so it's a model Synergy's implementation partner is likely already equipped to run.

## Conclusion & recommendation
Approve the unified global HCM platform, delivered through a hybrid Waterfall-governed, Agile-executed programme — standardizing processes, improving data accuracy, and enhancing employee experience, with strong financial returns ($15.6M NPV, ~75% 3-year ROI, ~19-month payback) and a governance model suited to both board oversight and a 5-continent rollout. Recommended rollout start: Q3 2026.
